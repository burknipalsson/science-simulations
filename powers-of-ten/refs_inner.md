# Powers of Ten, inner rungs: reference numbers (verified 2026-10-17)

Tags:
- **VERIFIED** means I read the number in the primary paper, its PubMed abstract, or an authoritative data page (NIST, CODATA, WMO, the church's own site).
- **SECONDARY** means I read it in a reliable paper, review or reference page that cites the primary source.
- **UNVERIFIED** means I could not confirm it.
- **My calculation** marks numbers I derived myself.

## Corrections to the brief (read first)
- **Hallgrímskirkja tower:** the church's own site says **73 m**. The 74.5 m figure comes from Wikipedia and travel guides.
- **Water geometry:**
  - 0.9572 Å / 104.52° is the classic Benedict et al. geometry, used by TIP4P/2005. It is not Hoy & Bunker 1979.
  - Hoy & Bunker's r_e is 0.958 Å and θ_e is 104.48° (NIST CCCBDB).
- **Water dipole (Clough 1973):** 1.8546 D is the ground-vibrational-state value. The same abstract gives 1.8473 D as the "equilibrium" value.
- **TIP4P/2005 density:** about 0.997 g/cm³ at 298 K, not 0.993. The 0.993 figure is real water at 37 °C.
- **Standing adult:** frontal area is about 0.43 m² and CdA about 0.5 m², not 0.6–0.7 m².
- **Králík & Novotný 2003:** published in *Variability and Evolution* 11:5, not *Forensic Sci Int*. The 0.48 / 0.43 mm male/female ridge-breadth pair is UNVERIFIED.
- **Sweat-pore spacing:** measured at about 340 µm, roughly 29 pores per cm of ridge. Locard's "9–18/cm" could not be found.
- **Hb size "6.4 × 5.5 × 5.0 nm (Perutz)":** not found in any source I could read. The crystal structure gives about 6.5 × 5.7 × 5.4 nm (my calculation, below).

## 1. Nailfold capillaroscopy (healthy adults)

**Caveat:** the EULAR standardisation paper (Smith et al. 2020, Autoimmun Rev 19:102458, PMID 31927087) has no numbers in its abstract. The full text was paywalled or behind bot walls. So the EULAR thresholds below are SECONDARY, read in open-access papers by the same group (Smith, Cutolo) that cite it.

**Density**
- **EULAR cut-off for normal density (SECONDARY):** ≥7 capillaries per linear mm, counted in the distal row.
  - Bergkamp, Smith et al. 2023, Quant Imaging Med Surg 13:3938, citing Smith 2019: "The current cut-off value for a normal capillary density in healthy adults is seven capillaries per mm". https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10240033/
  - Hysa, Cutolo et al. 2023, RMD Open 9:e003071. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10351269/
  - Komai et al. 2024, Nutrients 16:1914, Table 1. It also lists older normal ranges of 6–14, 7–12, 9–13 and 9–14 per mm (mean about 10). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11206784/
  - An Egyptian consensus uses <9/mm as "low" instead (El Miedany et al. 2023, Arch Rheumatol 38:451). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10689008/
- **Measured density in healthy people (VERIFIED):**
  - 9.7 ± 0.8 loops/mm in 30 healthy controls (Arvanitaki et al. 2021, Respir Res 22:27). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7819216/
  - 7.0 ± 1.2/mm by capillaroscopy and 6.8 ± 1.5/mm by OCT angiography, n = 13 (Dong et al. 2022, Quant Imaging Med Surg 12:1844). https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/85174/html
  - Children: 6.7 ± 0.8/mm, range 4–11 (Bergkamp 2023).

**Loop dimensions**
- **Healthy adult loops, n = 30 (VERIFIED, Arvanitaki 2021 Table 2):**

| Measure | Value (µm) |
|---|---|
| Arterial limb width | 7.4 ± 1.3 |
| Venous limb width | 8.8 ± 1.4 |
| Apical loop width | 11.5 ± 2.3 |
| Total capillary width | 29.5 ± 4.3 |
| Length | 313 ± 84 |

  - These widths are of the red-cell column, which is what capillaroscopy sees.
- **True lumen versus red-cell column (VERIFIED, abstract, Mahler et al. 1983, Int J Microcirc Clin Exp 2:147, PMID 6432718):** the red-cell column is 10.8 ± 3.0 µm wide on the arteriolar side and 12.0 ± 2.7 µm on the venular side. FITC-albumin shows the true lumen is about 4.2–4.6 µm (38%) wider, because of the plasma layer next to the wall.
- **Older limb diameters (SECONDARY, BioNumbers 115130, citing Butti et al. 1975, Microvasc Res 10:220):** about 9–12 µm in the arteriolar limb and about 12–18 µm at the venous end. https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=115130
- **Visible loop length (SECONDARY, Komai 2024):** 200–250 µm in some reports, up to about 500 µm in others. "Elongated" is defined variously as more than 300, 500 or 700 µm.
- **Intercapillary distance (SECONDARY, El Miedany 2023):** normal is <110 µm. A gap of more than 500 µm is an avascular ("drop-out") area.

**Shape, orientation and depth**
- **Shape (SECONDARY, Komai 2024; Gorasiya et al. 2022, Indian Dermatol Online J 13:600):** hairpin or inverted "U" loops, with arterial and venous limbs joined at the apex. Tortuous and crossing loops are normal variants. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9595142/
- **Orientation:**
  - In the distal row the loops lie parallel to the skin surface, which is why the whole loop can be seen there (SECONDARY, Shore 2000, Br J Clin Pharmacol 50:501). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2015012/
  - The capillaries "run parallel to the skin surface" (VERIFIED, Bourquard et al. 2018, Sci Rep 8:5301). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5871877/
  - The loops usually point perpendicular to the nail edge, toward the nail (Komai 2024).
- **Depth:**
  - The capillaries are "superficial, i.e., 50–100-µm deep" (VERIFIED as Bourquard 2018's statement, but not a primary measurement).
  - A primary depth measurement in healthy people: UNVERIFIED (none found).
  - Optical capillaroscopy reaches only a few tens of µm in bright-field (Aguirre et al. 2018, Photoacoustics 10:31). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6032507/

**Instrument**
- **Magnification and field of view:**
  - The gold-standard protocol is ×200 videocapillaroscopy, recording "at least two adjacent fields of 1 mm in the middle of the nailfold" on fingers 2–5 (VERIFIED in El Miedany 2023 and in Arvanitaki 2021's methods).
  - Shore 2000 describes TV microscopy at 120–400× final magnification.
  - Bourquard 2018 used a 5× objective with a 1360 × 1088 µm field at 60 frames per second.

**Clinical definitions**
- **Dilated and giant capillaries (SECONDARY, Arvanitaki 2021, Hysa 2023 and Komai 2024, all citing Smith 2019/2020):**
  - Normal: each limb under 20 µm.
  - Dilated ("irregularly enlarged"): apical limb over 20 µm and under 50 µm.
  - Giant: homogeneously enlarged, apical limb ≥50 µm.
  - Older non-EULAR rule: dilated if the arterial limb is over 15 µm or the venous limb over 20 µm.
- **Scleroderma patterns (VERIFIED, abstract, Cutolo, Sulli, Pizzorni, Accardo 2000, J Rheumatol 27:155, PMID 10648032):** three stages are named.
  - Early: few giant capillaries and haemorrhages, density preserved.
  - Active: frequent giant capillaries and haemorrhages, moderate loss of capillaries.
  - Late: severe loss of capillaries, disorganisation, and ramified ("bushy") capillaries.
  - The wording on density comes from Nagy et al. 2021, Diagnostics 11:1606 (SECONDARY).
- **Fast-track algorithm (Smith et al. 2019, Autoimmun Rev 18:102394, PMID 31520797):**
  - VERIFIED from the abstract: it sorts images into "non-scleroderma" or "scleroderma pattern". Agreement with the principal expert was κ = 0.96 for 135 trainees and κ = 0.94 for 85.
  - SECONDARY for the rule itself: scleroderma pattern means giant capillaries, or reduced density (≤3/mm) together with abnormal shapes (Öztürk 2026, Balkan Med J 43:360; Hysa 2023).

## 2. Red-cell velocity in resting nailfold capillaries

**Velocity at rest**
- **Fagrell, Fronek & Intaglietta 1977 (VERIFIED, abstract, Am J Physiol 233:H318, PMID 888975):**
  - 0.65 ± 0.3 mm/s at rest, at a skin temperature of 30.4 °C, in 12 healthy subjects.
  - The velocity swings spontaneously 6–10 times a minute, and every capillary shows a pulsatile component.
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/888975/
- **Butti et al. 1975 (SECONDARY, BioNumbers 115132):** mostly 1–2 mm/s, maximum about 2.5 mm/s. Flow is faster in the arterial limb than in the venous limb. The cells were tracked by the pattern of plasma gaps between red cells. https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=115132
- **Bollinger et al. 1974 (Microvasc Res 7:61, PMID 4206888):** the citation is confirmed, but there is no abstract, so its values are UNVERIFIED.
- **Healthy controls in later studies (VERIFIED, abstracts):**
  - Median 671 µm/s, range 29–4421; local cooling lowered it to 221 µm/s (Hahn & Shore 1994, J Physiol 478:109, PMID 7965826).
  - 910 ± 240 µm/s at 22 °C, against 230 ± 310 µm/s in scleroderma (Hahn et al. 1998, J Invest Dermatol 110:982, PMID 9620310).
  - 425 µm/s (range 290–800) and 680 µm/s (range 140–1090) in two healthy groups (Hahn, Jünger & Shore 2004, Clin Hemorheol Microcirc 31:227, PMID 15322328).
  - 0.83–0.90 mm/s at rest, falling to about 0.2–0.3 mm/s after cooling (Martina et al. 1999, Microvasc Res 57:94, PMID 10049657; these were hypertensive patients).
  - 56–685 µm/s measured along six capillaries (Shih et al. 2011, Microvasc Res 81:68, PMID 21047523).
- **Arterial versus venous limb (VERIFIED, Dremin et al. 2025, J Biomech 179:112482, one volunteer, 150 frames per second):** the arterial limb is about twice as fast as the venous limb. One loop ranged from 0.6 to 2.6 mm/s over 80 s. https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/eprint/47028/
- **Modelling value (VERIFIED as stated, Bourquard 2018):** 100–1000 µm/s, taken as 800 µm/s. White cells pass one at a time, showing as optical gaps in the red-cell column.
- **Value to use on the page:** about 0.5–1 mm/s at rest, with large swings. Cooling slows it to about 0.2 mm/s, and Raynaud's attacks can stop it.

**Haematocrit and spacing**
- **Tube haematocrit and red-cell spacing in human nailfold capillaries:** UNVERIFIED (no direct measurement found).
- **Related numbers:**
  - The red-cell column fills about 67–68% of the capillary diameter (SECONDARY, Shore 2000).
  - A longitudinal "RBC fraction" of 77 ± 1% has been measured, but under the tongue, not at the nailfold (VERIFIED, Machin et al. 2017, J Rheumatol 44:1603).

**Capillary pressure (bonus)**
- **Landis 1930 (Heart 15:209) (SECONDARY):** arteriolar limb 32 mmHg, loop apex 20 mmHg, venous limb 12 mmHg.
  - I read these numbers in Fahr & Ershler 1937 (Proc Soc Exp Biol Med 37:701), who measured 33 / 18 / 12 mmHg themselves. https://www.ebm-journal.org/journals/experimental-biology-and-medicine/articles/10.3181/00379727-37-9700/full
- **Modern servo-nulling measurements at the loop apex, heart level (VERIFIED):**
  - 18.9 ± 4.9 mmHg (Shore et al. 1993, Am J Physiol 265:H820, PMID 8214115).
  - 16.7 ± 3.7 mmHg (Hahn & Shore 1994).
  - 19.1 ± 3.7 mmHg (Hahn et al. 1998).

## 3. Fingertip skin

**Epidermis**
- **Fingertip pad epidermis (VERIFIED, abstract, Jones et al. 2025, Clin Anat, doi 10.1002/ca.24292, PMID 40464477):**
  - OCT on 53 healthy adults gives a 95% reference interval of **0.37–0.79 mm** for total epidermal thickness.
  - Thickness falls from thumb to little finger and is thinner in women.
  - Sweat-duct density is 30–86 per mm².
  - https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40464477
- **Index fingertip layers (VERIFIED, Infante et al. 2023, Int J Mol Sci 24:7121, Table 1):**
  - Stratum corneum 128–190 µm (OCT).
  - Viable epidermis 60–105 µm (laser-scan microscopy).
  - The sum is about 0.2–0.3 mm, thinner than Jones 2025. The difference is probably method and site, so cite each study separately.
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10138989
- **Fruhstorfer et al. 2000 (Clin Anat 13:429, PMID 11111894) (VERIFIED, qualitative only):** the horny layer is thickest on the thumb, thinner toward the little finger, thinner at the sides of ridges and thinner in women. The abstract gives no numbers.
- **Epidermis at the nailfold / proximal nail fold:** UNVERIFIED (no primary measurement found). The page can say it is thinner than the fingertip pad, but give no number.

**Fingerprint ridges and pores**
- **Ridge density (VERIFIED, review tables in Sharma et al. 2021, Acta Biomed 92:e2021366, from Gutiérrez-Redomero et al. 2008, Forensic Sci Int 180:17):** ridges per 25 mm² square. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8689342

| Area | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Radial | 16.2 ± 1.4 | 17.9 ± 1.5 |
| Ulnar | 15.3 | 16.4 |
| Proximal | about 12 | about 12 |

- **Sex thresholds (VERIFIED, abstract, Acree 1999, Forensic Sci Int 102:35):** ≤11 ridges per 25 mm² suggests a male print, ≥12 a female print. https://europepmc.org/article/MED/10423851
- **Mean ridge breadth (SECONDARY):** about **0.45 mm** for adults aged 40 and over, from Burchill et al. 2023 (MethodsX 11:102292) citing Králík & Novotný 2003 (Variability and Evolution 11:5). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10428107/
  - **My calculation:** 16 ridges across a 5 mm square gives 5/16 ≈ 0.31 mm spacing measured perpendicular to the ridges, at the radial area.
  - Ridge-count density and ridge breadth are defined differently, so the two numbers are not directly comparable.
  - The 0.48 / 0.43 mm male/female pair: UNVERIFIED.
- **Pore spacing along a ridge (VERIFIED, Schmid et al. 2024, Biomedicines 12:2768):**
  - SEM gives a mean pore-to-pore distance of **340 ± 39 µm**, about 29 pores per cm.
  - The controls were about 13 years old.
  - Pore diameter is quoted as about 66–287 µm.
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11673990
  - Locard's "9–18 pores/cm": UNVERIFIED.

**Nail**
- **Nail plate thickness by ultrasound, 27 healthy adults (VERIFIED, Bellinato et al. 2023, Arch Dermatol Res 315:593, Table 1):** thumb 0.48 mm, index finger 0.46–0.47 mm, little finger 0.41–0.42 mm, with SD about 0.04. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10020276
- **Thumbnail free edge by calipers (VERIFIED, abstract, Altan Ferhatoğlu et al. 2018, Int J Dermatol 57:1253):** 0.75 ± 0.17 mm in non-pregnant women.
- **Eponychium and cuticle (SECONDARY, Wikipedia citing Treuting 2018):** the eponychium, or proximal nail fold, is the thickened living skin at the nail base. The cuticle is the dead keratinised layer it sheds onto the nail plate. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponychium

## 4. Inside the red cell

**Indices**
- **MedlinePlus "RBC indices" (VERIFIED):**
  - MCHC 32–36 g/dL.
  - MCH 27–33 pg (men) and 26–33 pg (women).
  - MCV 79–95 fL.
  - https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003648.htm
- **Mohandas & Gallagher 2008 (VERIFIED, Blood 112:3939):** typical red cell is 90 fL and 140 µm², about 40% more surface than a sphere of the same volume. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582001/

**The haemoglobin molecule**
- **Molar mass:**
  - **My calculation, from UniProt P69905 and P68871:** mature α is about 15,127 Da and β about 15,867 Da; with 4 haems (616.5 Da each) the α₂β₂ tetramer is about **64,450 Da**.
  - SECONDARY: 64,500, and 64,455 by mass spectrometry.
  - Write "≈64.5 kDa".
- **Dimensions:**
  - **My calculation from the deoxy-Hb crystal structure (PDB 2HHB, principal axes):** 65 × 57 × 54 Å between atom centres, about 68 × 60 × 57 Å including van der Waals radii. Radius of gyration is 23.5 Å.
  - **Effective sizes (VERIFIED, Doster & Longeville 2007):** hard-sphere radius 28 Å and hydrodynamic radius 31 Å, i.e. about 6 nm across.
  - "6.4 × 5.5 × 5.0 nm": UNVERIFIED.
- **Molecules per cell:**
  - 2.7 × 10⁸ (SECONDARY, BioNumbers 102740, citing Pierigè 2008). https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=102740
  - **My calculation:** 30 pg ÷ 64,450 g/mol × N_A = 2.8 × 10⁸. The MCH range 26–33 pg gives 2.4–3.1 × 10⁸.

**Crowding**
- **Volume fraction (VERIFIED, Doster & Longeville 2007, Biophys J 93:1360):**
  - At the physiological 330 mg/mL, the dry-protein volume fraction is Φ = 0.247.
  - Counting the hydration shell (0.35 g water per g protein), Φ is about 0.36.
  - So about a quarter of the cell's inside is Hb protein, and about a third with its bound water.
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1929019
- **Spacing:**
  - **My calculation:** 330 g/L is 5.1 mM, or 3.1 × 10²⁴ molecules per m³. The mean centre-to-centre spacing is then about 6.9 nm, leaving gaps of about 1 nm (roughly 3 water molecules).
  - **Doster 2007 (VERIFIED):** "an average center-to-center distance of 7 nm and a protein diameter of 6 nm".
- **Hb self-diffusion inside red cells (VERIFIED, Doster & Longeville 2007, neutron spin echo):**
  - At 37 °C, D = 1.75 ± 0.2 × 10⁻⁷ cm²/s (17.5 µm²/s).
  - Corrected to 20 °C in water, D = 1.1 × 10⁻⁷ cm²/s, which is 6.2 times slower than in dilute solution.
  - A Hb solution at the same concentration gives 2.5 × 10⁻⁷ cm²/s.
  - Caveat: neutron spin echo measures short-time, nanosecond-scale diffusion.
- **Longeville & Stingaciu 2017 (VERIFIED, Sci Rep 7:10448):** 1.75 × 10⁻⁷ cm²/s in red cells at 37 °C. The infinite-dilution value is 10.1 × 10⁻⁷ cm²/s. Other red-cell estimates quoted range from 0.9 to 2.2 × 10⁻⁷ cm²/s. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5585185
- **Not checked:** Riveros-Moreno & Wittenberg 1972 (J Biol Chem 247:895) values are UNVERIFIED. I did not look up NMR or FRAP values.
- **What the diffusion means (my calculation, D = 17.5 µm²/s):**
  - The rms 3D distance in 1 s is √(6Dt) ≈ 10 µm, about one cell diameter.
  - Moving one neighbour spacing (7 nm) takes about 0.5 µs.

**Membrane: lipid bilayer**
- A red-cell-specific bilayer thickness: UNVERIFIED.
- **Closest measured values (SECONDARY, BioNumbers 105821, Mitra et al. 2004 PNAS 101:4083, rat liver plasma membranes):** phosphate-to-phosphate thickness of 3.6–4.3 nm. https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=105821
- **Wording:** "≈4–5 nm bilayer with a ≈3 nm oily core" is fair at textbook level.
- Avoid BioNumbers' "7.5 nm red cell membrane". It comes from dielectric modelling, not a structural measurement.

**Membrane: spectrin skeleton**
- **Liu, Derick & Palek 1987 (VERIFIED, abstract only, J Cell Biol 104:527):**
  - In spread skeletons the lattice is mostly hexagonal, with about 3% pentagons and 8% heptagons.
  - 84% of crosslinks are single tetramers.
  - Globules of 9–12 nm sit about 80 nm from the distal tetramer ends.
  - The "~76 nm junction-to-junction" figure is not in the abstract: UNVERIFIED.
  - https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.104.3.527
- **Byers & Branton 1985 (VERIFIED, PNAS 82:6153):** spread skeletons show 200-nm-long tetramers. Junctions carry 37 nm actin rods, with 5–6 tetramers per junction. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC391010
- **Native mouse skeleton by cryo-ET (VERIFIED, Nans, Mohandas & Stokes 2011, Biophys J 101:2341):**
  - Spectrin contour length between junctions is 46 ± 15 nm (range 19–97 nm), against about 190 nm fully extended.
  - Each junction has 4.2 ± 1.0 connections.
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3218374
- **Native human skeleton by STORM (VERIFIED, Pan et al. 2018, Cell Rep 22:1151):** junction-to-junction distance is about 80 nm. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2017.12.107
- **What to say:** native mesh spacing is disputed, roughly 46 nm (cryo-ET, mouse) to 80 nm (STORM, human). Artificially spread skeletons show about 200 nm.
- **Lattice estimate (my calculation):** 120k tetramers × 2 ends ÷ 6 per junction ≈ 40,000 junctions. 140 µm² ÷ 40,000 gives a triangular-lattice spacing of about 64 nm, between the two native measurements.

**Membrane: protein copy numbers**
- **Spectrin:**
  - "There are 240,000 spectrin dimers per red cell", i.e. about 120,000 tetramers (VERIFIED, Korsgren, Peters & Lux 2010, J Biol Chem 285:4757). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836081/
  - 1 × 10⁵ tetramers and 5 × 10⁵ actin per cell (VERIFIED, abstract, Pinder & Gratzer 1983, J Cell Biol 96:768).
- **Band 3:**
  - Estimates range from 0.9 to 1.6 × 10⁶ per cell (VERIFIED, Korsgren 2010).
  - 1.2 × 10⁶ (SECONDARY, BioNumbers 101805).
  - **My calculation:** about 8,600 per µm², one every 11 nm.
- **Junctional complexes:** I found no direct count (UNVERIFIED). My estimate is about 40,000–48,000 (lattice calculation above).

## 5. Water at 37 °C (310.15 K)

**Real water**
- **Density (VERIFIED, NIST Chemistry WebBook, IAPWS-95, 1 atm):** **0.993330 g/cm³**. Viscosity is 0.6913 mPa·s. https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/fluid.cgi?T=310.15&PLow=0.101325&PHigh=0.101325&Digits=6&ID=C7732185&Action=Load&Type=IsoTherm&TUnit=K&PUnit=MPa&DUnit=g%2Fml&VisUnit=uPa*s
- **Self-diffusion (VERIFIED, Mills 1973, J Phys Chem 77:685, Table III, full PDF read):** 2.299 at 25 °C, 2.919 at 35 °C and 3.575 at 45 °C, in units of 10⁻⁹ m²/s. https://fenix.ciencias.ulisboa.pt/downloadFile/2533412229372806/Mills1973.pdf
  - **My calculation:** Arrhenius interpolation gives **3.04 × 10⁻⁹ m²/s at 37 °C**.
  - Holz, Heil & Sacco 2000 (PCCP 2:4740): the citation is confirmed, but the table was paywalled, so its values are UNVERIFIED. Its 298 K value of 2.299 is SECONDARY, quoted in arXiv:2411.12545.
- **First O–O peak (VERIFIED, abstract, Skinner et al. 2013, J Chem Phys 138:074506, X-ray, ambient):** position **2.80(1) Å**, height 2.57(5). doi 10.1063/1.4790861
  - Skinner et al. 2014 (J Chem Phys 141:214507) covers 254–366 K. The O–O coordination number at 3.30 Å is 4.3 at all temperatures. The abstract gives no first-peak shift at 37 °C. doi 10.1063/1.4902412
- **Hydrogen bonds per molecule (SECONDARY):**
  - About 3.58 "inferred from experimental data" (arXiv:1801.08893).
  - Six definitions applied to SPC/E give 3.37–3.95 (Prada-Gracia et al. 2013, arXiv:1305.3060).
  - Say "about 3.5, depending on definition; 4 in ice".

**TIP4P/2005 model**
- **Abascal & Vega 2005 (VERIFIED, abstract, J Chem Phys 123:234505):** density maximum at 278 K. Densities at 1 bar differ from experiment by 7 × 10⁻⁴ g/cm³ on average. doi 10.1063/1.2121687
- **Density along 1 bar (VERIFIED, Pi et al. 2009, Mol Phys 107:365, Table 1):** 1.0002 at 283 K, 0.9987 at 294.4 K, 0.9972 g/cm³ at 300 K. The table stops at 300 K. https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0905.4009
  - TIP4P/2005 at 310 K: no published value (UNVERIFIED). Expect about 0.993–0.994 g/cm³ given the 7 × 10⁻⁴ accuracy (my inference).
- **Self-diffusion:**
  - 2.08 × 10⁻⁹ m²/s at 298 K: UNVERIFIED (I could not open the original table).
  - 2.49 ± 0.06 × 10⁻⁹ m²/s at 300 K after the finite-size correction (VERIFIED, Tazi et al. 2012, arXiv:1204.2501). https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1204.2501
- **Vega & Abascal 2011 (VERIFIED, abstract, PCCP 13:19663):** the 17-property test scores TIP4P/2005 at 7.2/10, against SPC/E 5.1, TIP4P 4.7, TIP5P 3.7 and TIP3P 2.7.

## 6. The water molecule

- **Dipole (VERIFIED, abstract, Clough, Beers, Klein & Rothman 1973, J Chem Phys 59:2254):** ground-state μ = **1.8546 ± 0.0006 D**. The "equilibrium" value is 1.8473 ± 0.0010 D. doi 10.1063/1.1680328
- **Dipole by molecular beam (VERIFIED, abstract, Shostak, Ebenstein & Muenter 1991, J Chem Phys 94:5875):** μ = 1.85704 + 0.00508(v₁+½) − 0.03166(v₂+½) + 0.02246(v₃+½) D.
  - **My calculation:** the ground state gives 1.8550 D.
  - NIST CCCBDB lists 1.857 D.
- **Equilibrium geometry (VERIFIED, NIST CCCBDB, citing Hoy & Bunker 1979):** r_e = 0.958 Å, θ_e = 104.4776°. https://cccbdb.nist.gov/exp2x.asp?casno=7732185
- **Classic geometry (SECONDARY):** 0.9572 ± 0.0003 Å and 104.52 ± 0.05° (Benedict et al.), quoted in IUCr 2021 and Fletcher 1971. This is the geometry TIP4P/2005 uses. https://journals.iucr.org/a/issues/2021/a2/00/a60478/a60478.pdf

## 7. The proton

**Measured charge radius**

- **Muonic hydrogen:**
  - **Pohl et al. 2010 (VERIFIED, abstract, Nature 466:213):** 0.84184(67) fm, 5.0σ from the CODATA value of the time (0.8768 fm). doi 10.1038/nature09250
  - **Antognini et al. 2013 (VERIFIED, abstract, Science 339:417):** **0.84087(39) fm**. Magnetic radius 0.87(6) fm, Zemach radius 1.082(37) fm. doi 10.1126/science.1230016

- **Bezginov et al. 2019 (VERIFIED, abstract, Science 365:1007):** 0.833 ± 0.010 fm, from the Lamb shift in ordinary hydrogen. doi 10.1126/science.aau7807

**Shape implied by the dipole form factor (my calculation)**
- G(Q²) = (1 + Q²/Λ²)⁻² is the Fourier transform of ρ(r) ∝ e^(−Λr), so the charge density is exponential.
- The mean square radius is ⟨r²⟩ = 4!/(2!·Λ²) = 12/Λ².
- With Λ² = 0.71 GeV² and ħc = 0.197327 GeV·fm, Λ = 4.270 fm⁻¹.
- So r_rms = √12/Λ = **0.811 fm**, about 3.5% below the modern 0.841 fm.
- The dipole is a good overall shape but not exact. For a picture, use ρ ∝ e^(−r/a) with a = r_rms/√12 ≈ 0.243 fm, taking r_rms = 0.841.

**The proton radius puzzle**
- Largely resolved toward the small radius (SECONDARY, PDG).
- Muonic hydrogen, PRad, Bezginov and CODATA 2018/2022 all give about 0.84 fm.
- PDG still notes "some tension" among a few measurements.

## 8. Wind around a standing person

**Comfort**
- **Penwarden 1973 (Building Science 8:259) (SECONDARY, abstract text via search snippet):** discomfort starts at a mean wind of 5 m/s, above 10 m/s it is definitely unpleasant, and above 20 m/s it can be dangerous. The paper gives no drag coefficient. https://swov.nl/en/publicatie/acceptable-wind-speeds-towns

**Drag and frontal area**
- **Drag on a walking adult (VERIFIED, Mesquita et al. 2025, Multidisciplinary Biomech J, doi 10.46298/mbj.14554, Table 1; treadmill in a wind tunnel, 8 men):**
  - Walking into a 5 m/s headwind: Cd = 1.15 ± 0.05, CdA = **0.54 ± 0.05 m²**.
  - All walking and running conditions: Cd 1.01–1.15, CdA 0.44–0.54 m².
  - https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/bitstreams/08429213-c31d-49d4-b961-71a647e2e199/download
- **Frontal area (VERIFIED, same paper):** A = 0.139 h² while moving. Davies 1980 gives 0.142 h² for a standing subject (SECONDARY).
  - **My calculation:** for h = 1.75 m, A ≈ 0.43 m².
  - Hoerner's standing-man value: UNVERIFIED (not read).

**Roughness lengths**
- **Davenport–Wieringa classes (VERIFIED, WMO WIGOS code list, following WMO-No. 8):** https://codes.wmo.int/wmdr/SurfaceRoughnessDavenport

| Class | Example terrain | z0 |

  - The urban revision is Davenport, Grimmond, Oke & Wieringa 2000 (AMS 12th Conf. Applied Climatology, paper 4b.2).

**Wind at 1.5 m (my calculation)**
- Formula: u(1.5) = u(10)·ln(1.5/z0)/ln(10/z0), with κ = 0.40, a neutral profile and no displacement height. Starting from 6 m/s at 10 m:

| z0 | u* | Wind at 1.5 m |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0002 m | 0.22 m/s | 4.95 m/s |
| 0.03 m | 0.41 m/s | **4.0 m/s** (67%) |
| 0.5 m | 0.80 m/s | 2.2 m/s (37%) |
| 1.0 m | 1.04 m/s | 1.1 m/s (18%) |

- **Caveat:** in a city, 1.5 m is inside the canopy and roughness sublayer, where the log law does not hold. The proper form uses ln((z−d)/z0), with displacement height d ≈ 0.5–0.8 × building height, and is valid only above the rooftops.
- Street-level wind near a tall building can be faster than the log law predicts, because of channelling and wake gusts. This is Penwarden's original concern.
- For an open hilltop plaza in front of the church, z0 ≈ 0.03–0.1 m (about 4 m/s at head height) is a defensible choice, provided the page says it is approximate.

**Drag force (my calculation)**
- F = ½ρU²·CdA, with ρ = 1.225 kg/m³ and CdA = 0.5 m².
- 4.9 N at 4 m/s and 11 N at 6 m/s.
