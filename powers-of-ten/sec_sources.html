Scale truth

What’s real in each layer

Nothing on this page is drawn by eye. Every layer is either a simulation from this series or measured data, placed in one chain of coordinate frames: the box of universe, the Galaxy, the solar system, the spinning Earth, a street corner, a person, a fingertip, a capillary, a red cell, a water molecule, a proton. The chain was checked against JPL before any graphics were drawn. Seen from Hallgrímskirkja at 13:00 UTC on 16 October 2026, it puts the Sun at 16.803° altitude, 176.573° azimuth, against JPL Horizons’ 16.803°, 176.578°, and the Moon (below the horizon, near new) at a distance of 406,478.3 km against JPL’s 406,478.5 km.

Cosmic web2.1 million dark-matter particles grown from z = 50 to today by the particle-mesh engine of The Cosmic Web (306.6 Mpc box, 400 steps, ΛCDM with Planck-like parameters). The Local Group is placed where the simulation has a matching spot: 3.8×10¹² suns within 1 Mpc, a mildly dense sheet, and a cluster of 8.8×10¹⁴ suns 16.4 Mpc away. The Galaxy is turned so that cluster lies toward the real Virgo cluster.The power spectrum matches linear theory on large scales (within 11% at k = 0.14 Mpc⁻¹).
Local Group291 galaxies within 4 Mpc at their measured positions and distances (Local Volume Database, Pace 2024), plus the large ones it omits. Andromeda and Triangulum are tilted by their measured position angles and inclinations.Andromeda at 765 kpc from the Sun.
Milky WayA star-particle model built from measured structure: exponential disc (scale length 2.6 kpc), a bar 5 kpc in half-length at 27°, and the seven spiral arms fitted to radio-maser parallaxes by Reid et al. (2019). It turns on a flat 236 km/s rotation curve. Young stars are born in the arm pattern, drift and fade. The 155 globular clusters are real (Harris catalogue).The arms’ tangent longitudes come out within 0.3° of Reid’s fits: 337.1°, 327.7°, 306.3° and 285.9°.
Nearby stars6,100 real stars from the HYG catalogue (Hipparcos, Yale Bright Star, Gliese): everything within 25 pc, and luminous stars to 600 pc, each moving on its measured space velocity.Catalogue distances and velocities.
Solar systemPlanet, Earth–Moon barycentre and Moon states at the landing moment from JPL Horizons, carried forward and back on Kepler orbits.After one sidereal year the Earth returns to within 2,100 km of its start.
EarthThe WGS84 ellipsoid, oriented by precession and sidereal time, lit by the Sun’s true direction. Surface: NASA Blue Marble Next Generation for October, at 500 m resolution over Iceland. Night side: NASA Black Marble city lights. Reykjavík: Sentinel-2 cloudless 2024 at 10 m.The Sun’s and Moon’s positions in the sky above Reykjavík, against JPL (above).
Street levelBare ground from ArcticDEM (a 2 m stereo survey; buildings masked out and filled, trees removed by a morphological opening). 2,910 buildings from OpenStreetMap, standing on that ground at their tagged heights, or at heights measured from the ArcticDEM surface where untagged. Hallgrímskirkja is OSM’s 3D model: about 100 parts, the stepped basalt-column wings included. The ground is drawn from OSM’s roads, paths and lawns over Sentinel-2 colour. Shadows come from the Sun’s true direction.Ground at the landing point 39.8 m above sea level. Tower 73 m (the church gives 73 m).
Person and windAn adult of average proportions (1.75 m) in a jacket. The wind is the forecast for this hour, 6.0 m/s from 226° at 10 m (Open-Meteo), brought down to head height with a log profile (z₀ = 0.1 m): 3.5 m/s. The air around the body is The Wind Tunnel’s lattice-Boltzmann solver on 17 million cells 1 cm apart. 5,750 recorded smoke paths replay in a seamless loop.Drag area of a person facing a uniform wind: 0.546 m²; wind-tunnel measurement 0.54 ± 0.05 m² (Mesquita 2025). Side-on in today’s wind: 0.13 m², about 1 N.
Nail foldA row of hairpin capillaries 60 µm under the skin, nine per millimetre, 0.3 mm long. Arterial limbs are 7.4 µm, venous 8.8 µm and the apex 11.5 µm, from capillaroscopy of healthy adults. The loops’ exact positions and wiggles are random. Blood moves through them in real time at the recorded speed.Resting red-cell speed in the nail fold: 0.65 ± 0.3 mm/s (Fagrell 1977).
Red cellsRecorded from the fine-grid engine of Inside a Drop of Blood: a 7.4 µm vessel 48 µm long (periodic), plasma on a 0.25 µm lattice, and four cells with 2,562-point Skalak membranes and a viscosity contrast of 5. Recorded for 60 ms after the flow settled; shown as their 642-point outer meshes.Cells 0.60 mm/s, 1.19 × the mean flow; tube haematocrit 0.18.
MembraneA patch at the front of the followed cell, moving with it. Band 3 at 8,600 per µm². The spectrin net is a jittered triangular lattice 64 nm across, about 15 nm under a 4.5 nm bilayer. Copy numbers are measured (Korsgren 2010); individual positions are random.Native mesh spacing is disputed, from 46 nm (cryo-ET) to 80 nm (STORM).
HaemoglobinThe 1.25 Å crystal structure of oxygenated human haemoglobin (PDB 2DN1), 4,524 heavy atoms, packed at random positions and orientations at 33 g/dL with no atomic overlap. Molecules nearer the eye than about 60% of the way to the target are cut away so the view can reach inside.Protein volume fraction 0.244 (about 0.25 measured in red cells).
WaterThe Hydrogen Bond’s TIP4P/2005 engine, 512 molecules at 310 K. 2 ps recorded every 10 fs, played forward then back: Newton’s laws run equally well in reverse. Kept out of the protein.O–O peak 2.83 Å (X-ray 2.80 Å); 3.6 H-bonds each; ρ 0.985 g/cm³ (real 0.993); D 2.6 × 10⁻⁹ m²/s (real 3.0).
Electron cloudThe followed molecule’s electron density from coupled-cluster theory (CCSD/aug-cc-pVDZ, computed with PySCF), at the same geometry as the water model. Fourteen natural orbitals, evaluated exactly in the shader at every scale. The glow follows the logarithm of the density; the faint shells are at 0.002 and 0.12 electrons per bohr³.Dipole 1.853 D; measured 1.855 D (Clough 1973). The shader matches PySCF to 4 parts in 10¹⁵.
ProtonThe charge density implied by the proton’s dipole form factor, an exponential with rms radius 0.8408 fm (muonic hydrogen, Antognini 2013; CODATA 2022 0.84075 fm). Quarks and gluons are not drawn.Size is measured; the shape is the textbook model.
Side trip: Sgr A*The 40 S-stars on the Keplerian orbits fitted by Gillessen et al. (2017), moving from their positions today. The nuclear star cluster is random stars with its measured half-light radius (4.2 pc) and flattening (Schödel et al. 2014). Sgr A* is 4.15 million suns (GRAVITY 2019), ray-traced with Event Horizon’s exact Schwarzschild light-bending; its glowing disk is illustrative.S2’s pericentre falls at 2018.33 (observed 2018.38), 119 AU from the hole. Shadow 52.1 µas (EHT 2022: 51.8 ± 2.3 µas).
Time trip: AndromedaThe two galaxies’ centres follow a semi-analytic orbit: Hernquist dark haloes (1.0 and 1.5 × 10¹² suns) and Chandrasekhar dynamical friction, from today’s velocity (109 km/s toward us, 57 km/s sideways: van der Marel 2012, 2019). 60,000 stars are test particles in both galaxies’ gravity, stepped live in the page as in Bridges & Tails.With van der Marel 2012’s inputs the model gives a first pass at 4.1 Gyr and a merger (centres within 15 kpc for good) at 5.7 Gyr; their N-body runs gave 3.9 and 5.9 Gyr.
