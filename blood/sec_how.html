How it works

A fluid, some elastic bags, and a handshake

lattice Boltzmann · 0.25–0.5 µm cells

The plasma

Space is divided into a grid of cubes a quarter or half a micrometre on a side, up to a few million of them. In each, 19 numbers track how much fluid is moving in each of 19 directions; every time step they stream to the neighbouring cube and relax toward equilibrium. That simple rule reproduces the Navier–Stokes equations of a viscous fluid. Walls bounce the fluid back at their exact curved position.

642 or 2,562 points per cell

The cells

Each cell is a closed mesh of triangles with the measured elasticity of red-cell membrane: a shear modulus of 5.3 µN/m, a strong resistance to changing area, a bending stiffness of 2×10⁻¹⁹ J, and a fixed volume. The resting biconcave shape is the one it relaxes back to. Inside, the cytoplasm is five times more viscous than plasma.

the immersed-boundary method

The handshake

The membrane and the fluid never share a grid. Each step, every membrane point spreads its elastic force onto the nearby fluid cubes; the fluid moves; then every point moves at the fluid's local speed. Membrane and plasma move together, so the cell carries its contents and the plasma flows around it.

why time is slowed

Keeping it watchable

At this size, flow is so slow and viscous that only ratios matter: how hard the flow pulls compared with how stiff the cell is. The simulation keeps those ratios but lets the plasma carry about a thousand times more inertia than real blood, which lets each step cover more time. The flow is still dominated by viscosity, and quartering the inertia changed the results by under 1%.

every step, on your graphics card

Seven passes per step

Measure each cell's area and volume; compute the force on every membrane point; find nearby points of other cells (so cells never pass through each other); spread the forces into the fluid; advance the fluid; and move the membranes. A fast graphics card does this thousands of times a second.

what is left out

Limits

No membrane viscosity, no sticking to the vessel wall, no clumping into rouleaux, no living machinery: a white cell's crawling and a parasite's knobs are beyond it. The sickled cells keep the stiffness measured for oxygenated ones; fully deoxygenated cells are far stiffer than this model can step stably.
