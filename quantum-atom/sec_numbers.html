Honest numbers

How close is this to nature?

Every simulation on this page was checked against an exact result or a measurement before any graphics were drawn, using the same code that runs here. The quantum world is a good place for this, because its simplest cases can be solved exactly.

The electron waves (the main stage and the tunnelling lab) use the split-operator method: the wave is advanced alternately in position space, where the walls act, and in momentum space, where its motion is exact, with a fast Fourier transform carrying it between the two. It conserves probability by construction. On the graphics card one step, two Fourier transforms of a million-point grid, takes about a third of a millisecond, and the result agrees with an independent double-precision version on the CPU.

The hydrogen atom is drawn from its exact solution: Laguerre polynomials for the distance from the proton, spherical harmonics for the direction. The decay rates come from the exact overlap of each pair of orbitals. H₂⁺ is solved exactly too, in coordinates shaped like rugby balls around the two protons, where its equation separates into two simple ones. MRI uses the Bloch equations, the standard description of how proton spins tip, dephase and recover.

What is left out: the main stage is two-dimensional, the electron's spin and relativity are ignored (they shift hydrogen's lines by about one part in 70,000), the protons are held still while the electron moves, and the which-slit detector is an ideal, instantaneous measurement.
